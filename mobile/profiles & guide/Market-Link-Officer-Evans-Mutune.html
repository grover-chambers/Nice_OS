Eastern Corridor

Eastlands holds more small outlets per street than anywhere else you will work. Your risk is not finding shops — it is counting the same shop twice. Work one side of the street all the way out, then the other side all the way back. Never criss-cross. In Utawala and Mihang’o the estates are new and shops open every month, so ask how long they have been open.

Local knowledge for your patch

People here buy flour in 1kg and 2kg packs, often daily. When you ask about monthly volume, help them get there: how many bales in a normal week, then multiply together.

Say something before you burn out

Six days of walking, week after week, is genuinely hard. Weeks four and five are where people start missing counts and getting sloppy without noticing. If you are exhausted, tell your lead early. A planned lighter Saturday costs the programme almost nothing; someone quitting in week five costs it a great deal.

What you record at every shop

Eight fields · all of them

Location

GPS pin captured while standing at the door

Identity

Shop name and type — duka, kiosk, mini-market, supermarket, wholesaler, eatery, posho mill

Contact

Owner or manager name and a working phone number

Proof

Shopfront photo with the signage legible

Shelf

Flour brands stocked and how many facings each — Nice, Unga, Pembe, Capwell, other

Volume

Monthly offtake in bales

Supply

Who supplies them — distributor, wholesaler, or they buy direct

Landmark

Nearest landmark, street or estate name, written out

The two rules that void a record

No pin, no record. No photo, no record. A shop without a GPS pin or a shopfront photo does not count toward your daily total — not because we are being difficult, but because a record nobody can verify is worth nothing to the client and nothing to us.

How to open the conversation

Interaction guidelines
Say something close to this
“Habari yako. My name is Evans, I work with Market Link. We are mapping shops in this area so suppliers can serve you better. It takes about three minutes — is now okay, or should I come back?”

Greet in whatever language they greet you in. Give them a way to say no — people who feel cornered refuse, and people who are asked properly usually agree. If they say come back, write down when and actually come back.

Do ALWAYS

  • Greet first, in whatever language they greet you in. Then your name, then Market Link.
  • Say plainly what you are doing: mapping shops so suppliers can serve them better. Three or four minutes of their time.
  • Ask before you photograph. Every time.
  • Wait if they are serving a customer. Never interrupt a sale — it is the fastest way to be thrown out.
  • Say thank you and leave well, whether they helped you or not. You may need to come back.
  • Carry your ID and wear the vest. People relax when they can see who you are.

Never NO EXCEPTIONS

  • Never promise supply, stock, credit, a distributor visit, or a better price. You are not selling and you cannot commit anyone.
  • Never criticise Unga, Pembe, Capwell or any brand in front of an owner. You are counting, not campaigning.
  • Never argue about price, politics or anything else. Record what they say and move on.
  • Never ask for or accept money, goods or a discount from a shop. Not even a soda.
  • Never enter a back room, store or house. Do your work at the counter.
  • Never invent a record. A guessed shop is worse than a missing one, and it will be found.

The one thing worth repeating
